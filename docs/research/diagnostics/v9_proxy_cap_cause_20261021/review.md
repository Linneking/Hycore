# Offline analyzer review — 2026-10-22

Scope: read-only source review of `analyze.py`, `counterfactuals.py`, their local tests, the observer, and the V9/V8 mapping and loss sources. No server access, GPU use, production edit, or new training was performed by this review.

No material arithmetic defect was found in the reviewed analyzer or one-step counterfactuals.

- Dynamic inside/outside cohorts use the raw parameter norm **before each update**. The aggregation sums per-proxy arrays and weights summary means by cohort count, so pooled statistics describe proxy-update observations, rather than a mean of unequal step means. They do not describe a fixed set of proxy identities.
- The signed radial gradient is `dot(g, p/||p||)`: a negative value supports outward motion under ordinary gradient descent. Actual AdamW norm change is separately measured; the code does not substitute gradient sign for optimizer displacement. The algebraic Adam components share the actual updated second-moment denominator.
- The fixed-choice reconstruction uses recorded pair/triple IDs and recorded masked hinge activity, preserves division by all sampled triplets, and applies the actual `to_ball` mapping with GS. The proxy-loss reconstruction keeps both distance-matrix endpoints attached to the same proxy parameter. It therefore includes both endpoint and ancestor-candidate roles.
- Counterfactuals restore the saved current gradient, historical first/second moments, step/bias correction, and hyperparameters, and validate a native one-step CPU replay against the recorded update before exporting alternatives. Removing radial historical `m` means projection in raw parameter coordinates before Adam's coordinatewise preconditioner; it does not remove every possible radial contribution of historical `m` after preconditioning. Zeroing historical `m` while retaining `v` and step is not a fresh optimizer.

Reporting boundaries that matter:

1. `full_st - fixed_choice` subtracts a CPU-reconstructed fixed-choice gradient from the saved actual graph gradient. Recorded IDs/activity avoid routing and hinge-selection changes, but a small CPU/GPU distance discrepancy does not itself bound derivative discrepancy. Call this the recorded-ST minus fixed-choice **reconstruction residual**, or identify it as an approximate routing contribution. The identity residual in the JSON is algebraically zero by construction and is not an independent validation of a pure ST decomposition. Exact separation would require the fixed-choice VJP on the same actual training graph/device.
2. The diagnostic's epoch-1 cap count 229 differs from historical V9's 196. Matching initialization and source contracts plus the small single-process HIER observation test do not establish a bitwise replay of the complete DDP encoder training. Original V9 performs an extra first-step parameter audit at specified epochs; the diagnostic disables it. That audit restores cloned cloud, BN and RNG, but its `no_sync`/distributed execution is outside the single-process observer test. Cross-device numerical paths, input/runtime identity, and the skipped audit remain possible sources of divergence; this review has not attributed the discrepancy to any one of them. The bounded run supports a mechanism reproduction, not an exact reconstruction of the historical trajectory.
3. One-step eps/history interventions are conditional on the native run's saved state and fixed current gradient. They can establish an immediate displacement change; they cannot establish long-run cap avoidance, improved hierarchy, or classification benefit.

These qualifications do not invalidate the observed optimizer accounting or the source-level facts that the objective lacks an explicit ancestor depth ordering and the cap acts in the forward map rather than projecting the stored parameters and Adam state.
